## Fountain hook

Status: verified. Valve's [The International Archives film](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d6H-HEpnlk8) contains direct accounts from participants and the original match footage. At 0:26 it discusses choosing Pudge and Chen. At 1:47 the player describes asking Puppey to teleport him while he tried a hook. At 3:25 the film explicitly discusses hooking TongFu players with Chen teleport. The draft frame at 0:29.5 shows The International, upper-bracket round 2A, game 3 of 3, Natus Vincere against TongFu, and Pudge and Chen among Na'Vi's picks. The match footage at 1:43 shows the hooked opponent arriving in Na'Vi's base.

Valve's [original match broadcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OaGSi1YTA-E) has the same teams and round in its title and was uploaded in August 2013. Its commentary assigns Chen to Puppey at 10:16 and Pudge to Dendi at 10:29. The first fountain kill appears around 26:05. The broadcast identifies the combination at The International at 26:28. The contemporaneous [joinDOTA interview with TongFu's Hao](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B3T2lVtxjLQ) names The International 2013 in its title and describes Na'Vi's fountain-hook strategy in its own description. These original records establish the TI3 date, independent of the later film's upload date.

Evidence: `flashback-primary/fountain-draft.png`, `fountain-103.png`, the three corresponding compact metadata files, and the two Valve `en-orig.vtt` caption files. I opened the draft and base-arrival frames and read the relevant captions. Auto captions contain spelling errors, so hero and team spellings come from the on-screen draft and the source titles.

The film also contains the spoken question "is that balanced" around 4:16. The current eyebrow can remain. There is no need to restore the removed Aegis, balance-change, or other incidental claims.

## Sheever's nickname

Status: verified through two primary sources, with the author connection recorded as an inference between them. In [PGL's own nickname interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rgU06QNNmfE&t=18s), the first interviewee explicitly gives her gamertag as Sheever. At 0:18-0:31 she says she needed a World of Warcraft name, opened a book at a random page, found a sea serpent named Shreever and removed the R. This is first-person testimony. It does not name the author in the inspected passage.

The publisher's [Spanish book excerpt](https://www.penguinlibros.com/ar/fantasia/149400-libro-las-naves-de-la-locura-las-leyes-del-mar-2-las-leyes-del-mar-2-9789506443542/fragmento) identifies Robin Hobb as author and names Shreever among the sea serpents. The publisher's [English Mad Ship page](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/80906/mad-ship-by-robin-hobb/) separately identifies the book as the second volume of the Liveship Traders series. Joining the book text to her named character supports the Robin Hobb answer. The existing alternatives, Tolkien, Martin and Sanderson, do not match the named book character. Remove the explanation's Liquipedia attribution and cite the interview plus publisher excerpt. A shorter explanation can omit the series title without losing the fact checked by the question.

Evidence: `flashback-primary/rgU06QNNmfE.metadata.json`, `rgU06QNNmfE.en-orig.vtt`, `sheever-name-26.png`, and `hobb-publisher-excerpt.html`. The frame confirms that the relevant passage is an on-camera PGL interview. Speaker attribution comes from the spoken gamertag and source context, not face recognition. The source join should stay explicit in the ledger.
